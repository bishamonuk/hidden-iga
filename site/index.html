Why Iga

Quiet valleys, close to everything.

Iga sits in Mie Prefecture, in the hills between Osaka, Kyoto and Nara. It is best known as the home of the Iga ninja and the birthplace of the haiku poet Bashō — and for rice fields, tea, sake and Iga-yaki pottery.

The rural and mountain districts around the city are full of large old houses that families no longer live in. Iga City runs its own Akiya Bank to find them new owners, and publishes inspection notes, floor plans and video tours for each home.

  • Railway lines into Osaka, Nagoya and Kyoto
  • Large plots — often with a garden, field or storehouse
  • Honest condition notes from the city's own survey

Fees

One clear fee: 10% of the price.

Our buyer support fee is 10% of the purchase price, payable on completion. It covers English support from your first enquiry to moving in.

Other costs paid directly by the buyer: the licensed agent's brokerage fee (capped by Japanese law), registration and scrivener fees, real estate acquisition tax, stamp duty and fixed asset tax. We give you an estimate before you make an offer.

FAQ

Good questions

Can a foreigner buy a house in Japan?

Yes. Japan does not restrict foreign nationals or non-residents from owning residential property, and you do not need a visa to buy. Owning a house does not give you the right to live in Japan — residence depends on your visa. 【要確認】

Can I buy the fields that come with a house?

Farmland is different. Buying or transferring it needs permission under Japan's Agricultural Land Act, which is usually given only to people who will farm it. Several listings note this — we check each case with the city and the agent.

What condition are the houses in?

Many are old and need work — the city lists every known issue, from roof leaks to unusable toilets. We translate these notes in full, and can arrange an inspection and renovation quotes before you commit.

Do I need to travel to Japan?

We recommend at least one visit. Viewings can start by video, and much of the paperwork can be done remotely, but signing and payment may need you or a representative in Japan.

Who is the seller?

Private owners. The Iga City Akiya Bank publishes the homes, and a licensed real estate agent handles each sale. We are your English-speaking guide through the process.
